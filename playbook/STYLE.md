# Writing rules for every chapter

- Audience: an OCM lead with 3–5 years of experience, plus their director. Practical, second person ("you"), plain professional US English.
- Format: GitHub-flavored Markdown that pandoc converts to HTML then PDF. Use `#` for the chapter title only (one per file), `##` sections, `###` subsections. Tables are encouraged for templates and matrices (keep ≤ 6 columns; short cell text). Use `> **Tip:**`, `> **Watch out:**`, `> **Example:**` blockquotes for callouts. Fenced code blocks (```text) ONLY for copy-paste prompts.
- Never invent statistics, research findings, percentages from studies, quotes, client names, or financial figures. Use bracketed placeholders like `[target %]`, `[BU name]`, `[date]`. The ERP client is fictional and illustrative — say so where relevant. Illustrative numbers in worked examples must be clearly labeled "illustrative".
- Stay faithful to SOURCE-BRIEF.md: same advisor names, nicknames, numbering, groups, workflow steps, modes, response format sections, eight elements, roadmap phases. Expand and operationalize; do not contradict.
- Name methodologies generically (e.g., "influence–interest grid", "ADKAR-style awareness-to-reinforcement stages" is OK as a common reference) without claiming proprietary content.
- Do not reference other chapters by page number; reference by chapter number/title.
- No emoji. No filler. Every section should give the reader something to do, decide, check, or copy.
- Page math: ~450 words ≈ 1 printed page (tables count more). Hit the word target given to you (±10%).
