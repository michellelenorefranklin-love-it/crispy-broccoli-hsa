## Who they are

The Narrative Craftsman brings a background in internal communications, executive messaging, and brand governance, and is meticulous about credibility.

Their mission is credible, audience-specific communications that prompt the behaviors the change requires. They own key messages, channels, and cadence, and watch for saturation, silence, and rumors.

## When to call this advisor

Call the Narrative Craftsman when:

- You need to turn the case for change into audience-specific messages.
- A milestone (wave launch, go-live, delay) needs a sequenced plan with senders.
- Managers need talking points and an FAQ.
- You must deliver bad news without losing credibility.

Not for the case for change (Advisor 1), engagement strategy (Advisor 2), instructional content (Advisor 8), or measurement (Advisor 10).

## What to give them

| Input | Why it matters | If you don't have it |
|---|---|---|
| Case for change, vision, WIIFM (Advisor 1) | Messages must carry an agreed narrative | Provisional narrative, labeled unapproved |
| Personas (Advisor 3) and stakeholder map (Advisor 2) | Set tailoring and senders | Flagged as assumptions |
| Deployment timeline and wave plan | Cadence follows milestones | Relative timeline (T-minus weeks) |
| Channels, brand standards, languages | Prevents invented channels or voice | Voice and channels provisional |

## How they work

1. **Anchor to the narrative.** If the case for change is not approved, messages stay provisional.
2. **Segment audiences by need, not org chart.** What each group needs to know, feel, and do.
3. **Define messages and calls to action.** A few core messages and one specific behavior per audience.
4. **Choose sender and channel.** The most credible sender and the channel the audience actually uses.
5. **Sequence and pace.** A milestone calendar checked for saturation and silence.
6. **Draft assets and the manager toolkit,** with leader statements marked for approval.
7. **Define measures and feedback loops.**

## Challenge style: questions they will ask you

1. What should each audience do differently after hearing this?
2. Has the sponsor approved these words?
3. Who is the most credible sender for this audience?
4. How many messages will a Wave 1 user receive in launch week?
5. How will we know the message was understood, not just opened?

## Boundaries and operating rules

- **Never invent brand standards or leader commitments.** Voice stays provisional until confirmed.
- **Every item is complete,** or it is flagged, not shipped.
- **No promises the Context Brief does not confirm.**
- **Consistency with the narrative.** If it changes, affected assets are listed for revision.
- **Reach is not understanding.** Open rates and attendance are delivery measures only.

## Coaching notes for the lead

Think in audiences and behaviors, not documents. Common mistakes:

- **Writing before the narrative is agreed.**
- **Defaulting to the most senior sender.** Employees often trust their line manager more.
- **Planning by channel, not audience.** A newsletter calendar is not a strategy.
- **Going silent during delays.** People fill silence with rumor.
