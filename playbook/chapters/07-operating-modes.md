# 7. Four Operating Modes

Start simple, with one advisor, then expand.

| Mode | Who is involved | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Solo advisor | One expert | Draft a specific deliverable |
| 2. Cross-functional | Two to four advisors | Tackle a shared problem |
| 3. Board challenge | Several advisors, independently | Stress-test a strategy or recommendation |
| 4. End-to-end engagement | The whole board, coordinated by the Chair | Guide discovery to strategy, design, deployment, and sustained adoption |

In every mode the Charter and Context Brief apply, and you decide.

## Mode 1: Solo advisor

**When to use it.** One deliverable in one discipline, such as a stakeholder map, or when testing a persona.

**How to set it up.** Name the advisor, deliverable, audience, and decision, and ask for Board consultation.

```text
Act as the [Advisor number and name]. Using Context Brief v[x.x], develop a
[deliverable] for [scope/audience]. It will be used to [decision or purpose]
by [date]. Ask me up to [three] questions before you draft. Respond in the
standard format and include Board consultation.
```

Source example: "Act as the Change Impact Advisor and develop an impact assessment for a global ERP implementation."

**Expected output.** A recommendation, a first draft with placeholders, labeled assumptions, risks, and reviewers.

**Pitfalls.** Treating one advisor's answer to a multi-discipline question as complete; ignoring Board consultation; not checking assumptions.

> **Tip:** Run each advisor solo at least once before combining them.

## Mode 2: Cross-functional

**When to use it.** A problem genuinely spans two to four disciplines, such as a launch plan combining communications and champions.

**How to set it up.** Name the advisors, one lead author, and a question for each contributor. Ask the Chair to surface disagreements rather than blend them.

```text
Have the [Advisor A], [Advisor B], and [Advisor C] develop a [deliverable]
for [audience/problem], using Context Brief v[x.x]. [Advisor A] leads and
produces the integrated draft. [Advisor B]: focus on [question].
[Advisor C]: focus on [question]. Board Chair: synthesize, show where the
advisors disagree, and list the decisions I need to make.
```

Source example: "Have the Stakeholder, Employee Experience, and Communications advisors develop an engagement strategy for frontline employees."

**Expected output.** One integrated deliverable, disagreements with evidence, and decisions required.

**Pitfalls.** Calling more than four advisors; stapled documents instead of one draft; consensus that hides trade-offs. If advisors agree on everything, ask what each would have argued alone.

### Useful cross-functional combinations

| Shared problem | Advisors | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| Frontline engagement strategy | 2 Stakeholder, 3 Employee Experience, 7 Communications | 2 |
| Impact-driven training plan | 5 Change Impact, 8 Learning, 4 Readiness | 8 |
| Local launch mobilization | 6 Champions, 7 Communications, 2 Stakeholder | 6 |
| Go-live and stabilization plan | 4 Readiness, 9 Pilot and Hypercare, 10 Adoption Analytics | 9 |

## Mode 3: Board challenge

**When to use it.** Before a recommendation reaches an executive: go/no-go, a wave plan change, a budget request.

**How to set it up.** State your recommendation and all your evidence, including weak spots. Name the most exposed advisors and have each respond independently.

```text
Board challenge. My recommendation: [recommendation]. My evidence: [evidence].
[Advisors X, Y, Z]: respond independently. Each give the strongest case for,
the strongest case against, and the evidence that would resolve it. Do not
soften your view to agree with the others. Board Chair: synthesize, identify
whether the disagreements are about facts, assumptions, or risk appetite, and
frame the decision I should put to [executive/committee].
```

Source example: "Challenge my go-live readiness recommendation: strongest case for, strongest case against, and the evidence that would resolve it."

**Expected output.** Independent positions, settling evidence, and a decision framing (template in Chapter 8).

**Pitfalls.** Sharing only supporting evidence; treating the case against as a reason to abandon rather than a pointer to needed evidence; challenging small decisions.

> **Watch out:** A board challenge is not a vote; the dissent may hold the evidence that matters most.

## Mode 4: End-to-end engagement

**When to use it.** To guide a whole phase, especially at engagement start.

**How to set it up.** Complete the Context Brief as far as you can. Ask the Chair to sequence work by stage with explicit handoffs, milestones, and decisions. Then work one stage at a time using Modes 1 to 3.

```text
Board Chair: guide me through [period or phase] of this engagement, using
Context Brief v[x.x]. Sequence the work by stage, name the lead advisor for
each deliverable, show the handoffs between advisors, and identify the
milestones and decisions I need to prepare for. Flag what you need from me
before each stage begins.
```

Source example: "Guide me through the first 30 days of an OCM engagement."

**Expected output.** A staged plan with deliverables, leads, handoffs, and decision points. Chapter 19 applies this to the fictional ERP program.

**Pitfalls.** Treating illustrative week boundaries as fixed; asking for everything at once; expecting autonomy. Parallel execution and automated handoffs need an orchestration layer; in a prompt-only setup, you are the orchestrator.

## Mode selection guide

| If you need to... | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Produce one deliverable, or learn or test an advisor | Mode 1 | Fastest, clearest accountability |
| Solve a cross-discipline problem or keep deliverables consistent | Mode 2 | One draft with shared assumptions and handoffs |
| Pressure-test a recommendation or prepare a go/no-go | Mode 3 | Surfaces objections and the evidence that resolves them |
| Plan a phase, or re-plan after a major scope change | Mode 4 | Sequences deliverables, owners, and handoffs |

## Escalation path: from Mode 1 to Mode 4

| From | To | Escalate when |
|---|---|---|
| Mode 1 | Mode 2 | Board consultation names advisors whose input would change the recommendation |
| Mode 2 | Mode 3 | The output supports a significant executive decision, or disagreement affects risk |
| Mode 3 | Mode 4 | Several deliverables or the work sequence must change, or the brief has a major version change |
| Any mode | Back down | The question narrows to one discipline, or debate stops adding evidence |

A typical progression: Advisor 5 drafts an impact assessment (Mode 1), you add Advisors 8 and 4 (Mode 2), challenge the wave 1 readiness position with 4, 9, and 10 (Mode 3), and the Chair re-sequences when go-live moves (Mode 4).

## Mode checklist

- [ ] The Charter and current Context Brief are loaded.
- [ ] The advisors are named, with a lead where more than one is involved.
- [ ] The request states the decision or purpose the output supports.
- [ ] You chose the smallest mode that fits the question.
- [ ] You know how you will validate the output before it reaches the client.
