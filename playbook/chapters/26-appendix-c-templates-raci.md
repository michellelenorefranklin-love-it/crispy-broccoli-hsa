## How to read the RACI

The RACI matrix assigns four roles to each deliverable.

- **R — Responsible:** does the work of producing the deliverable.
- **A — Accountable:** owns the outcome and signs it off. Exactly one A per deliverable.
- **C — Consulted:** gives input before the deliverable is final; two-way.
- **I — Informed:** kept up to date; one-way.

Two rules from the human-in-the-loop operating model (see Chapter 2) shape every row.

1. **The OCM lead is Accountable for every OCM deliverable.** The lead validates, decides, and delivers. Where the client sponsor must approve, the lead remains accountable for the deliverable's quality, and the sponsor approves the decision it supports.
2. **The board is never Accountable.** The Board Chair and the advisors can be Responsible for a first draft and Consulted for challenge. They cannot own an outcome, approve anything, or speak to the client.

In the matrix, "R (draft)" means the board produces the first draft. The lead then validates it with real data and refines it, which is why the lead also carries R for the final version.

Notes on the matrix:

- **Sponsor approval is not accountability for the deliverable.** "Approves" and "Decides" record the client's decision rights. The lead still owns the quality of what is put in front of the sponsor.
- **The project or IT team owns its own processes.** For example, IT owns incident management in hypercare. The OCM deliverable interfaces with that process; it does not replace it.
- **Adjust to your engagement.** Replace "[Project/IT team]" with the real teams, and add columns for HR, works councils, or local leaders where relevant. Keep the matrix to six columns per table; split it if needed.

> **Watch out:** If any row shows the Board Chair or an advisor as "A", fix it before you share the matrix. It signals that judgment has been delegated to the board.

## Using the RACI with the client

1. Draft the matrix with the Board Chair, using your Context Brief.
2. Replace generic roles with named client roles. Do not put personal names in the board's working copy.
3. Review it with the client sponsor and the project lead in the first two weeks.
4. Record agreed decision rights in the Context Brief so every advisor uses them.
5. Revisit the matrix at each lifecycle stage gate, and when scope or the governance structure changes.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every deliverable has exactly one owning advisor in the index.
- [ ] Every RACI row has exactly one A, and it is the OCM lead.
- [ ] No board role is marked Accountable, approves, or decides.
- [ ] Client decision rights match the agreed governance.
- [ ] Project or IT ownership boundaries, such as incident management, are explicit.
