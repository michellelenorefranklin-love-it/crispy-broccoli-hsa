# 19. Use Case: The First 30 Days of a Global ERP Program

This chapter turns the Mode 4 prompt, "Guide me through the first 30 days of an OCM engagement," into a week-by-week plan you can adapt. The client is fictional and illustrative. Nothing here describes a real organization, and every number in the examples is illustrative.

## The scenario

You have just been assigned as OCM lead on a global ERP implementation. What you know on day one:

| Field | What you know |
|---|---|
| Client | A fictional global enterprise (referred to here as "the client") |
| Program | Global ERP implementation across multiple business units and countries |
| Deployment approach | Phased deployment; wave dates to be confirmed |
| Known challenge | Limited visibility into impacts, readiness, and stakeholder concerns |
| Your priority | OCM strategy and baseline assessments |
| Your role | OCM lead, human in the loop: you frame, validate, decide, and deliver |

The goal for the month is a single milestone: **the OCM strategy and baselines are ready for client review.** Everything in the plan below serves that milestone.

> **Watch out:** The week boundaries in this chapter are illustrative, not a fixed method. Real programs overlap phases, slip on access to data, and pull work forward when a sponsor asks a question early. Use the sequence and the handoffs; flex the calendar.

## The 30-day plan at a glance

| Weeks | Phase | Lead advisors | Core outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Understand | 1 Vision, 2 Stakeholder, 3 Experience | Case for change and WIIFM; stakeholder map; illustrative employee personas |
| 2–3 | Assess | 4 Readiness, 5 Impact | Change impact assessment and risk register; readiness framework and baseline |
| 3–4 | Plan | 6 Champions, 7 Comms, 8 Learning, 10 Analytics | Champion network; communications and training strategies; adoption measurement framework |
| End of week 4 | Milestone | Board Chair | Strategy and baselines ready for client review |

The Board Chair coordinates throughout. Any advisor can be called at any time: if a sponsor raises a go-live concern in week 1, bring in Advisor 9 then.

Two handoffs hold the plan together:

- **Change Impact feeds Training and Readiness.** Advisor 5's impact assessment becomes the input for Advisor 8's training needs analysis and Advisor 4's readiness criteria.
- **Stakeholder personas feed Communications.** The segments from Advisor 2 and the personas from Advisor 3 become the audience structure for Advisor 7's messaging matrix.

## Before day one: set up the board

1. **Start the Client Change Context Brief.** Fill in what you know and mark everything else as unknown or assumed. The Brief is the foundation layer of the guardrails (see Chapter 5). Version it from the start: v0.1.
2. **Load the Board Operating Charter and the standard response format.** Every advisor answers in the nine-section format: Recommendation, Rationale, Analysis, Deliverable, Risks, Decisions required, Success measures, Next actions, Board consultation.
3. **Confirm your data rules.** Decide what client information you will share with the board and how you will anonymize it (see Chapter 22).
4. **Open an assumption log.** Every assumption an advisor labels goes here, with an owner and a date by which you will confirm or retire it.

```text
Act as the Board Chair. I am starting a new OCM engagement on a global ERP
implementation for a client across multiple business units and countries,
with a phased deployment and wave dates still to be confirmed. Here is my
Client Change Context Brief v0.1: [paste]. Guide me through the first 30
days. For each week, tell me which advisors to involve, which operating
mode to use, what I need to bring, what outputs to expect, and where I must
validate with real client data before going further. Label every
assumption you make.
```

## Week 1: Understand the case and the people

**Lead advisors:** 1 Change Vision & Business Value Strategist, 2 Stakeholder Intelligence & Engagement Strategist, 3 Employee Experience & Change Journey Designer.

**Modes:** Solo advisor for first drafts; cross-functional for the joint view of stakeholders and personas.

### What you bring

- Existing program materials: charter, business case extracts, steering committee papers (anonymized as needed).
- A list of business units, countries, and functions in scope.
- Notes from your first sponsor and program-lead conversations.

### Activities and prompts

**Draft the case for change (Advisor 1, solo).** Use the source prompt and ask for the challenge explicitly.

```text
Act as the Change Vision & Business Value Strategist. Draft a case for
change for our global ERP program and challenge any benefit we cannot yet
evidence. Use only the program materials I have pasted below. For each
benefit, state whether it is evidenced, assumed, or missing, and propose
the baseline, target, and owner needed to prove it. Include draft WIIFM
statements for [list of groups] and label them provisional.
```

**Build the first stakeholder map (Advisor 2, solo).**

```text
Act as the Stakeholder Intelligence & Engagement Strategist. Build a
stakeholder map for the ERP program and flag where sponsor alignment looks
weakest. Use the org information and meeting notes below. Segment by
impact, influence, support, and resistance. Do not infer motives without
evidence; where you are guessing, say so and tell me what I would need to
learn to confirm it.
```

**Draft illustrative personas (Advisor 3, solo).**

```text
Act as the Employee Experience & Change Journey Designer. Create three
illustrative personas for ERP end users and map their journey from
awareness to sustained use. Label every trait as a hypothesis, and tell me
which ones I should validate first and with whom.
```

### Outputs by end of week 1

- Case for change v0.1 with an evidence status for every benefit
- Draft WIIFM statements (provisional)
- Stakeholder map v0.1 with sponsor-alignment flags
- Three illustrative personas, every trait labeled as hypothesis
- Updated assumption log

### Human validation checkpoint 1

This is workflow step 4: you validate with real data.

- [ ] Walk the case for change with the program sponsor or their delegate. Which benefits do they actually own?
- [ ] Review the stakeholder map with the program lead. Who is missing? Whose position is mislabeled?
- [ ] Schedule at least [number] short conversations with real employees in the persona groups to test the hypotheses.
- [ ] Update the Context Brief to v0.2 with confirmed facts; keep unconfirmed items in the assumption log.

> **Tip:** Do not share the stakeholder map with the client in its raw form. Influence and resistance labels are sensitive. Share the engagement approach, not the political analysis.

## Week 2: Finish understanding, start assessing

**Lead advisors:** 2 and 3 (refine), 5 Change Impact, Risk & Mitigation Architect, 4 Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert.

**Modes:** Cross-functional for personas and stakeholders; solo for the impact assessment.

### Activities and prompts

**Reconcile stakeholders and personas (Advisors 2 and 3, cross-functional).** This produces the audience structure that Communications will use later.

```text
Have the Stakeholder Intelligence & Engagement Strategist and the Employee
Experience & Change Journey Designer reconcile the stakeholder map v0.2 and
the persona set v0.2 below. Produce one audience segmentation that both can
stand behind, note where they disagree, and flag which segments are
underserved. This segmentation will be handed to the Communications
advisor, so make each segment's needs explicit.
```

**Start the impact assessment (Advisor 5, solo).** Begin with one function where you have the best process information. Finance is a common starting point in an ERP program.

```text
Act as the Change Impact, Risk & Mitigation Architect. Draft an impact
assessment for the finance function in a global ERP rollout, with a
starter risk register. Use the process and role information below. Keep
impacts, risks, and issues distinct. For each impact, state what changes,
who is affected, the response, the owner, and how we will verify it. Mark
every impact rating as provisional until I confirm it with process owners.
```

**Frame readiness (Advisor 4, solo).** Readiness design starts now so that criteria are ready when impacts are known.

```text
Act as the Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert. Design a
readiness assessment for a phased global rollout and propose go/no-go
criteria. Wave dates are not confirmed, so design the framework to work
by business unit, country, and wave. Do not equate training completion or
a positive survey with readiness. Tell me what evidence each criterion
requires.
```

### Outputs by end of week 2

- Unified audience segmentation (the handoff to Communications)
- Impact assessment v0.1 for the first function, plus starter risk register
- Readiness framework draft: criteria, scoring scale, thresholds, cadence

### Human validation checkpoint 2

- [ ] Validate impact ratings with at least one process owner per function assessed.
- [ ] Confirm the risk register separates risks from issues, and that every risk has a named owner on the client side.
- [ ] Check that readiness criteria are observable, not opinion-based.
- [ ] Update the Context Brief to v0.3. Confirm the deployment assumption all advisors are using (for example, the number of waves and the indicative timeline) so it is consistent.

> **Watch out:** This is the point where incoherence creeps in. If Advisor 4 assumes three waves over twelve months and Advisor 1's narrative still says "one global go-live," fix the Context Brief now and re-issue it to every advisor.

## Week 3: Complete the assessment, start planning

**Lead advisors:** 5 and 4 (complete), then 6 Change Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist and 8 Learning Strategy & Capability Development Architect.

**Modes:** Solo to extend the impact assessment; cross-functional for the two handoffs.

### Handoff 1: Change Impact to Training and Readiness

This handoff is the backbone of the month. Do it explicitly, in one prompt, so the board traces each training need and readiness criterion back to a specific impact.

```text
Have the Change Impact, Risk & Mitigation Architect hand off the validated
impact assessment v0.3 to the Learning Strategy & Capability Development
Architect and the Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert.
Learning: run a training needs analysis for ERP finance users from these
impacts and outline a train-the-trainer approach. Readiness: map each
high-rated impact to at least one readiness criterion and baseline
measure. Show traceability in a table: impact ID, affected role, training
need, readiness criterion. Flag any impact that has no response.
```

A traceability table might look like this (illustrative):

| Impact ID | Affected role | Training need | Readiness criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIN-03 (illustrative) | Accounts payable clerk | Three-way match in new system | Clerks complete a supervised match on test data |
| FIN-07 (illustrative) | Regional controller | New close calendar and approvals | Controllers run a mock close within [threshold] |
| FIN-11 (illustrative) | Budget holder | Self-service approvals | Approval backlog below [threshold] in pilot |

### Readiness baseline

Ask Advisor 4 to turn the framework into a baseline plan: who will be assessed, how, and when. The baseline itself needs real data from the client. The board designs the instrument; you collect the evidence.

### Champion network (Advisor 6)

```text
Act as the Change Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist. Recommend a
champion network model for a multi-country rollout and justify how you
sized it. Use the audience segmentation and stakeholder map attached. State
the time commitment you are assuming for champions and flag where it may
overload people. Do not apply a universal ratio.
```

### Outputs by end of week 3

- Impact assessment v0.3 across in-scope functions, with risk register and mitigation tracker
- Training needs analysis and train-the-trainer outline
- Readiness baseline plan and data collection approach
- Champion network design draft

### Human validation checkpoint 3

- [ ] Confirm the traceability table with the training lead and a business readiness owner on the client side.
- [ ] Test champion time commitments with line managers in at least one country.
- [ ] Check that every high-rated impact has a response and an owner.

## Week 4: Plan and prepare for client review

**Lead advisors:** 7 Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect, 8 (finalize), 10 Adoption Analytics & Change Insights Strategist, then the Board Chair.

**Modes:** Cross-functional for communications and measurement; board challenge on the full strategy; Board Chair synthesis.

### Handoff 2: Personas to Communications

```text
Act as the Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect. Using the
unified audience segmentation and personas below, draft a communications
strategy and messaging matrix for the first 90 days. For each item name
the audience, sender, channel, timing, call to action, and measure. Use a
provisional voice; do not invent brand standards or leader commitments.
Coordinate with the Change Champion advisor on what champions will carry
and flag any risk of message saturation.
```

### Adoption measurement framework (Advisor 10)

```text
Act as the Adoption Analytics & Change Insights Strategist. Build an
adoption measurement framework for this program, with leading and lagging
indicators linked to the case for change success measures and the
readiness criteria. For each metric state what it measures, how it is
calculated, its limits, and the action it triggers. Mark every baseline
as "to be collected" unless I have provided real data.
```

### Board challenge

Before you package anything, stress-test the whole strategy.

```text
Board challenge: here is the draft OCM strategy and baselines package.
Have each advisor independently stress-test it from their discipline. Give
the strongest case that this strategy is ready for client review, the
strongest case that it is not, and the evidence that would resolve the
difference. Check that all deliverables use the same assumptions from
Context Brief v0.4.
```

### Board Chair synthesis

Finally, ask the Board Chair to assemble the package and prepare the decisions the client must make.

```text
Act as the Board Chair. Synthesize the case for change, stakeholder
engagement approach, impact assessment, readiness framework and baseline
plan, champion network, communications and training strategies, and
adoption measurement framework into one OCM strategy summary for client
review. Resolve any conflicts between advisors and show how. List the
decisions we need from the sponsor, with options and a recommendation for
each. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions.
```

### Outputs by end of week 4

- Communications strategy and messaging matrix (provisional voice)
- Learning strategy and curriculum outline
- Adoption measurement framework with KPI definitions
- Integrated OCM strategy summary and decision list
- Context Brief v0.4 and a closed-out assumption log

### Human validation checkpoint 4

- [ ] Read every deliverable yourself. You own what goes to the client.
- [ ] Remove or anonymize anything sensitive, such as raw stakeholder positions.
- [ ] Confirm that no figure, baseline, or commitment appears without a source.
- [ ] Pre-wire the key decisions with the sponsor before the milestone review.

## Handoff and validation summary

| Week | Handoff or checkpoint | From | To | What you validate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Checkpoint 1 | Advisors 1–3 | You | Benefits, stakeholder positions, persona hypotheses |
| 2 | Audience segmentation | Advisors 2, 3 | Advisor 7 | Segments match real populations |
| 2 | Checkpoint 2 | Advisors 4, 5 | You | Impact ratings, risk owners, criteria |
| 3 | Impact to training and readiness | Advisor 5 | Advisors 8, 4 | Traceability with client owners |
| 3 | Checkpoint 3 | Advisors 6, 8, 4 | You | Champion load, training scope |
| 4 | Board challenge and synthesis | All, Board Chair | You | Coherence, decisions, sources |

## End-of-month milestone review

The milestone is the strategy and baselines ready for client review. Run it as a working session, not a document readout.

### Suggested agenda (90 minutes, adjust to your client)

| Time | Item | Owner | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 min | Objectives and what has changed since kickoff | OCM lead | Set context; confirm scope |
| 15 min | Case for change, vision, and success measures | OCM lead with sponsor | Confirm the why and how value will be proved |
| 15 min | Stakeholder engagement approach and audiences | OCM lead | Agree on priorities and sponsor roles |
| 15 min | Impact assessment and top risks | OCM lead with process owners | Confirm what changes and who owns mitigations |
| 10 min | Readiness framework and baseline plan | OCM lead | Agree on criteria and data collection |
| 10 min | Champions, communications, and training strategies | OCM lead | Confirm approach and resourcing asks |
| 5 min | Adoption measurement framework | OCM lead | Agree on what success looks like |
| 10 min | Decisions required and next 30 days | Sponsor | Make or schedule each decision |

### Decisions to request (examples)

- Approve the case for change and the owners of each success measure.
- Confirm sponsor and leadership roles in the engagement plan.
- Approve the readiness criteria and the baseline data collection.
- Approve champion time commitments and the nomination process.
- Confirm deployment wave assumptions so that all plans align. [date]

> **Example:** If wave dates are still unconfirmed at the review, present the strategy with an explicit planning assumption, such as "illustrative: three waves over [number] months," and ask the sponsor to confirm or replace it by [date]. Do not let the board's planning assumption become the client's commitment by default.

## After the review: feed the loop

The workflow's feedback loop returns new learning to the Context Brief. After the review:

1. Record every decision and every change of direction in the Context Brief and increment the version.
2. Re-issue the updated Brief to the board and ask each affected advisor to update its outputs consistently.
3. Retire or confirm assumptions in the log.
4. Plan the next 30 days with the Board Chair, typically moving into detailed design and build and preparation.

```text
Act as the Board Chair. Here is Context Brief v1.0, updated after the
client milestone review, with the decisions made and the changes requested.
Identify which deliverables are affected, ask each responsible advisor to
update its output consistently, and propose the plan for the next 30 days.
```
