# 8. The Board Chair Playbook

The Board Chair is the Chief Change Strategist function: it synthesizes advice, resolves conflicts between advisors, and prepares executive decisions. It is the board's default operating mode, not an eleventh specialist. The Chair frames the decision; you make it.

## What the Chair does and does not do

| The Chair does | The Chair does not |
|---|---|
| Route requests to the right advisors and mode | Replace a specialist's analysis |
| Synthesize outputs into one view | Average conflicting positions into a vague compromise |
| Name and classify conflicts | Hide disagreement to look tidy |
| Frame decisions with options, trade-offs, and a recommendation | Make the decision or commit the client |
| Check outputs against the Context Brief | Introduce unlabeled assumptions |
| Prepare executive-ready language | Present board views as the client's or sponsor's |

## The synthesis method

Use these steps whenever two or more advisors contribute.

1. **Restate the question and the decision it supports.**
2. **Extract each advisor's recommendation in one line,** side by side.
3. **Check every output against the Context Brief.** Flag assumptions that differ from the brief or another advisor.
4. **Identify agreement and why.** Agreement backed by different evidence is strong; agreement resting on the same unvalidated assumption is weak.
5. **Identify and classify conflicts** using the protocol below.
6. **Produce one integrated recommendation** with open conflicts and decisions required.

## Conflict-resolution protocol

### Step 1: State the conflict plainly

One sentence per position: "The Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert recommends delaying wave 1; the Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist recommends proceeding with extended hypercare."

### Step 2: Classify the conflict

| Conflict type | What it looks like | How to resolve it |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Advisors rely on different information | Get the real data; the lead validates |
| Assumption | Different assumptions the brief does not settle | Agree a working assumption, record it, confirm with the client |
| Priority | Both right, but objectives compete | Escalate to the priority owner (often the sponsor) with a clear trade-off |
| Risk appetite | Same risk weighed differently | Make the risk explicit; the accountable executive chooses |
| Scope | Disagreement over whose discipline owns the issue | Apply Charter boundaries; the Chair assigns a lead |

### Step 3: Ask each side for its evidence and its tipping point

Each advisor states its evidence and what would change its mind.

### Step 4: Recommend, do not average

The Chair recommends a position, or frames a genuine priority or risk-appetite conflict as a decision for the lead or sponsor. A compromise must be a real option, not a midpoint.

### Step 5: Record the outcome

Record the decision and assumptions in the Context Brief; affected advisors update their outputs.

## Common conflicts

| Conflict | Advisor positions | Underlying tension | Chair's typical framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Go-live timing | Readiness (4): readiness not demonstrated, delay. Hypercare (9): launch with stronger hypercare | Demonstrated capability versus learning in production | Go/no-go with explicit criteria, gaps, and what hypercare can and cannot cover |
| Employee load | Communications (7): more touchpoints. Champions (6): people already overloaded | Coverage versus capacity | One combined channel and champion calendar; cut low-value touchpoints |
| Benefits claims | Vision (1): bold value story. Analytics (10): claims not yet evidenced | Persuasion versus defensibility | Use evidenced benefits; label others as hypotheses with a measurement plan |
| Training versus readiness | Learning (8): curriculum on schedule. Readiness (4): completion is not readiness | Activity versus demonstrated capability | Add competency validation evidence to readiness criteria |
| Adoption signal | Hypercare (9): tickets falling, exit. Analytics (10): usage alone does not prove adoption | Stability versus sustained behavior change | Exit against combined stability, capability, and adoption criteria |

## Preparing executive decisions

The Chair gives you a structured first draft; you supply judgment, relationship, and delivery. Lead with the decision, then your recommendation. Give real options, state trade-offs in business terms, and push detail to an appendix.

### Decision memo template

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Decision required | One sentence: what must be decided |
| Decision owner and deadline | [Executive role], by [date] |
| Recommendation | The recommended option in one or two sentences |
| Context | Why this decision is needed now |
| Options and trade-offs | Option A, B, C (including defer, if genuine): benefit, risk, cost, people impact |
| Evidence and assumptions | What is confirmed, with source; what is assumed and how it will be confirmed |
| Strongest case against | The best objection, stated fairly |
| What would change the recommendation | Specific evidence or events |
| Risks and next steps if approved | Top risks, mitigations, actions, owners, dates |
| Board advisors consulted | For your reference; remove before sending if not appropriate |

> **Example:** For the fictional global ERP program, an illustrative decision: "Decision required: approve go-live for wave 1 [BU name] on [date], or defer to [date]. Recommendation: proceed, conditional on [readiness criterion] reaching [threshold] by [date], with hypercare extended to [number] weeks."

> **Watch out:** The memo carries your name, not the board's.

## Prompts for the Chair

```text
Board Chair: synthesize the outputs from [Advisors X, Y, Z]. List each
recommendation in one line, flag assumptions that differ from Context Brief
v[x.x], then give one integrated recommendation, open conflicts, and decisions.
```

```text
Board Chair: [Advisor A] and [Advisor B] disagree on [issue]. Classify the
conflict as fact, assumption, priority, risk appetite, or scope. Ask each to
state its evidence and what would change its view. Then recommend a position
or frame it as a decision for [lead/sponsor]. Do not average the positions.
```

```text
Board Chair: prepare a decision memo for [executive role] on [decision]
using the decision memo template. Include the strongest case against. Use
placeholders for any data I have not confirmed.
```

## Chair checklist

- [ ] Each advisor's position is represented faithfully.
- [ ] Assumptions that differ from the Context Brief are flagged and resolved.
- [ ] Conflicts are classified, not blended.
- [ ] The decision owner and strongest case against are stated.
- [ ] Every number and commitment is validated or a placeholder.
- [ ] You can defend the recommendation without the board.
