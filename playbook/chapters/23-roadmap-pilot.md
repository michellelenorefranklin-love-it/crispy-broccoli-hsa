# 23. Roadmap and Pilot Plan

This chapter moves the board from concept to routine practice and gives you the basis for the conversation with your director and sponsor. Start simple, then expand: prove one advisor before relying on ten, and prove the board with prompts before investing in orchestration.

## The five-phase roadmap

| Phase | Timing | Focus | Main output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Define and configure | Now | Build the foundations | Ten personas, charter, response standard, Context Brief |
| 2 Test the specialists | Now | Prove each advisor alone | Passing test logs for all ten personas |
| 3 Test collaboration | Next | Prove advisors together | Passing cross-advisor scenarios |
| 4 Establish reusable assets | Next | Make the board repeatable | Templates, scorecards, exemplar deliverables |
| 5 Introduce orchestration | Later | Automate where value justifies it | Business case and design, if approved |

### Phase 1: Define and configure (Now)

Finalize the ten personas, charter, response standard, and Context Brief template at v1.0, and confirm the approved tool and data rules with policy owners.

- [ ] Every persona has all eight elements, with element 8, operating rules, explicit; [reviewer role] has reviewed the charter.

### Phase 2: Test the specialists (Now)

Run the eight scenarios per persona (Chapter 21); fix, version, and retest.

- [ ] Every persona scores 3 or above on all measures, with no automatic failures and a second reviewer's sample.

### Phase 3: Test collaboration (Next)

Run the cross-advisor tests, test Board Chair synthesis and key handoffs, and simulate the first 30 days on the fictional ERP client (Chapter 19).

- [ ] All tests pass with consistent assumptions, and the Board Chair resolves conflicts explicitly.

### Phase 4: Establish reusable assets (Next)

Build templates, scorecards, and exemplars at [location], from the fictional simulation and, with permission and full anonymization, pilot work. Keep client-specific information separate from generic templates.

- [ ] Every asset has an owner and version, no client-identifying information, and feedback from [number] practitioners.

### Phase 5: Introduce orchestration (Later)

Assess whether autonomous delegation, parallel execution, persistent shared artifacts, automated handoffs, or scheduled monitoring justify the effort and risk. If so, design with human checkpoints and pilot on fictional data first. [Decision owner] decides on evidence from phases 1–4.

> **Watch out:** Automating an untested board automates its weaknesses.

### Objectives

Show whether the board improves deliverable and decision quality, speeds first drafts, keeps deliverables consistent, and accelerates the lead's growth; confirm the guardrails and data rules work in practice.

### Choosing the engagement

A fictional engagement carries no data risk; a real one shows true value. If real, prefer an early-stage engagement, a supportive sponsor, and a lead who completed phase 2.

## Success criteria

Agree criteria and baselines from comparable past work before starting.

| Measure | Indicator | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality | Reviewer rubric score; fabrication incidents reaching client | [target score]; zero | Review log |
| Consistency | Conflicting assumptions found at review | [target number] | Board Chair check, peer review |
| Practical value | Time to first draft; decisions supported by board options | [target vs baseline] | Time and decision logs |
| Growth | Lead and director assessment | [target improvement] | Before-and-after review |

> **Tip:** Keep measures few and logging light.

## Governance

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Pilot sponsor ([Director, OCM]) | Approves, removes blockers, makes the scale decision |
| Pilot lead (OCM lead) | Runs the board, validates outputs, delivers, keeps logs |
| Reviewer; policy contact; asset owner | Scores samples; confirms data handling and disclosure; maintains versions and library |

Weekly, the lead logs usage and the reviewer scores a sample; every [number] weeks, check in with the sponsor. The end-of-pilot review covers results per criterion, one example of clear value and one without, guardrail performance, and needed changes, then decides: stop, extend, or scale. Report any data incident immediately and pause use until resolved.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fabricated facts reach the client | Lead validation; reviewer sampling; automatic-failure rule |
| Client data shared inappropriately | Approved tools; pre-share checklist; anonymization routine |
| Over-reliance weakens the lead's judgment | Lead makes and records every decision; director coaching |
| Generic or inconsistent outputs | Complete, versioned Context Brief; persona testing; Board Chair checks |
| Pilot effort competes with delivery | Narrow scope; light logging; sponsor support |
