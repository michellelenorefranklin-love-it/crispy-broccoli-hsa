# 20. Lifecycle Plays: Discovery to Sustained Adoption

A play is a repeatable way to run one lifecycle stage with the board. This chapter gives five plays, one per stage in the Client Change Context Brief, each with objectives, lead advisors, deliverables, decision gates, a prompt, and exit criteria. Lead advisors are indicative: the Board Chair coordinates every stage, and any advisor can be called at any stage.

## How to use the plays

- **Start each stage by updating the Context Brief** with the stage, scope, decisions made, and what has changed.
- **Validate between prompts.** An output becomes the next input only after you check it against real data.
- **Treat decision gates as client decisions.** The board prepares options and a recommendation; the sponsor or steering committee decides.
- **Leave a stage only when exit criteria are met or consciously waived**, recording who agreed and why.

| Stage | Lead advisors | Signature outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and assessment | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | Shared understanding of why, who, and what changes |
| Strategy and design | 4, 6, 7, 8, 10 | An integrated OCM strategy with measures |
| Build and preparation | 6, 7, 8 | Assets, networks, and training ready to deploy |
| Pilot and deployment | 4, 9 | A safe, evidence-based launch decision |
| Hypercare and sustained adoption | 9, 10 | Stable operations and proven adoption |

## Play 1: Discovery and assessment

**Objectives:** establish why the change matters and how value will be proved; identify who is affected and where resistance may arise; build a traceable view of impacts and risks; set the readiness baseline.

**Lead advisors:** 1 Vision, 2 Stakeholder, 3 Employee Experience, 4 Readiness, 5 Change Impact.

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Validated with |
|---|---|---|
| Case for change, benefits map, WIIFM matrix (1); stakeholder map and influence–interest grid (2); personas and journey maps (3) | 1, 2, 3 | Sponsor, program lead, employees |
| Readiness framework and baseline plan (4); impact assessment, heatmap, risk register (5) | 4, 5 | Business unit leaders, process owners |

**Decision gates:** 1a, sponsor confirms the case for change and success-measure owners; 1b, program leadership confirms scope, deployment assumptions, and audiences.

```text
Act as the Board Chair. We are entering discovery and assessment on
[program description]. Here is Context Brief [version]. Route the work,
tell me the order, and list the real data I must collect before any
assessment counts as validated.
```

**Exit criteria:**

- [ ] Every benefit evidenced or labeled as an assumption with an owner.
- [ ] Stakeholder map reviewed; persona hypotheses tested with real employees.
- [ ] Impact assessment validated with process owners for every in-scope function.
- [ ] Readiness framework agreed; baseline collection under way; Context Brief updated.

## Play 2: Strategy and design

**Objectives:** turn discovery insight into one integrated OCM strategy, including readiness criteria by wave and the measurement framework, on shared scope and timeline assumptions.

**Lead advisors:** 4 Readiness, 6 Champions, 7 Communications, 8 Learning, 10 Adoption Analytics.

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness criteria and go/no-go approach (4); learning strategy and curriculum map (8) | 4, 8 | Impact assessment |
| Champion network design (6); messaging matrix (7); KPI dictionary (10) | 6, 7, 10 | Stakeholder map, personas, success measures |

**Decision gates:** 2a, sponsor approves the strategy and resourcing asks, such as champion time and training capacity; 2b, program leadership approves readiness criteria and go/no-go thresholds before build.

```text
Have the Communications, Change Champion, and Learning advisors develop a
coordinated plan for [wave or audience]. Show how communications,
champion activity, and training are sequenced, and flag any week where
employees face too many asks at once.
```

**Exit criteria:**

- [ ] All deliverables trace to discovery outputs, with no conflicting timeline, wave, or audience assumptions.
- [ ] Every success measure has a baseline plan, target, and owner.
- [ ] Readiness criteria observable and approved; sponsor has approved strategy and resourcing.

## Play 3: Build and preparation

**Objectives:** build the assets, mobilize champions, equip managers, and prepare for pilot and first deployment.

**Lead advisors:** 6 Champions, 7 Communications, 8 Learning, with 4 Readiness supporting.

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Validated with |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding pack and scorecard (6); asset drafts, manager toolkit, FAQ (7) | 6, 7 | Champions, managers, client comms team |
| Facilitator materials and train-the-trainer plan (8); readiness checkpoint (4) | 8, 4 | Client training lead, BU leaders |

**Decision gates:** 3a, client brand owners approve voice and assets (drafts stay provisional until then); 3b, training lead confirms materials match the configured system and local requirements, including legal or works-council checks.

```text
Act as the Learning Strategy & Capability Development Architect. Review
these facilitator materials against the impact assessment. Which impacts
are not covered, where may the method not fit a country or role, and how
will competence be demonstrated rather than assumed from attendance?
```

**Exit criteria:**

- [ ] Communications approved and scheduled; champions onboarded with escalation routes.
- [ ] Trainers prepared; materials tested against the real system.
- [ ] No unresolved critical readiness gaps for the pilot scope; Context Brief updated.

## Play 4: Pilot and deployment

**Objectives:** reduce uncertainty through a pilot, make evidence-based go/no-go decisions per wave, and prepare launch support.

**Lead advisors:** 4 Readiness, 9 Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare.

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot charter, launch checklist, hypercare model and severity definitions | 9 | Define what the pilot must prove; prepare launch and support |
| Readiness heatmap and go/no-go report | 4 | Show readiness by wave with evidence |

**Decision gates:** 4a, steering committee approves the pilot charter; 4b, go/no-go per wave on demonstrated readiness, not reported green status; 4c, scale-up decision against pilot success criteria.

```text
Board challenge: challenge my go-live readiness recommendation for [wave].
Give the strongest case for, the strongest case against, and the evidence
that would resolve it. Have the Readiness and Pilot, Deployment &
Hypercare advisors lead.
```

**Exit criteria:**

- [ ] Pilot criteria evaluated with real data; lessons logged.
- [ ] Go/no-go decision recorded by the accountable client leader.
- [ ] Launch checklist complete; hypercare team briefed on triage, severity, and exit criteria.

> **Watch out:** Technical go-live success is not proof of adoption. Keep people-readiness evidence separate in every go/no-go.

## Play 5: Hypercare and sustained adoption

**Objectives:** stabilize adoption; route people issues and technical defects correctly; measure whether the change is understood, adopted, and sustained; connect results to promised benefits.

**Lead advisors:** 9 Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare, 10 Adoption Analytics; Advisor 1 rejoins for the benefits story.

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hypercare triage log and lessons-learned log | 9 | Track and resolve post-launch friction |
| Adoption dashboard spec, insight-to-action log, benefits report | 10 (with 1) | Segment adoption; turn findings into owned actions; show benefits progress |

**Decision gates:** 5a, hypercare exit when agreed criteria are met; 5b, handover of adoption measures and reinforcement to business as usual; 5c, adjust later waves based on lessons learned.

```text
Have the Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare and Adoption Analytics advisors
review the hypercare data below from [wave]. Separate defects, knowledge
gaps, process failures, and access problems; recommend actions and
owners; and tell me whether we are on track for hypercare exit.
```

**Exit criteria:**

- [ ] Hypercare exit signed off by the accountable client leader.
- [ ] Adoption measures owned by the business, with a review cadence and reinforcement in place.
- [ ] Benefits tracking handed over with baselines, targets, and owners.
- [ ] Lessons recorded in the Context Brief and applied to later waves.

## Moving between plays

In phased deployments you may run Play 5 for wave 1 and Play 3 for wave 2. Keep one Context Brief with a section per wave, have the Board Chair check assumptions whenever a wave's status changes, and feed lessons into later plays before their gates.

```text
Act as the Board Chair. [Wave 1] is in hypercare and [wave 2] is in build
and preparation. Tell me where their plans conflict, which wave 1 lessons
should change wave 2 now, and which decisions I need to raise with the
sponsor.
```
