# 20. Lifecycle Plays: Discovery to Sustained Adoption

A play is a repeatable way to run one stage of the change lifecycle with the board. This chapter gives you five plays, one for each stage in the Client Change Context Brief:

1. Discovery and assessment
2. Strategy and design
3. Build and preparation
4. Pilot and deployment
5. Hypercare and sustained adoption

Each play follows the same structure: objectives, lead advisors, key deliverables, decision gates, prompts, and exit criteria. The lead advisors are indicative. The Board Chair coordinates every stage, and you can call any advisor at any stage when the situation calls for it.

## How to use the plays

- **Start each stage by updating the Context Brief.** Record the stage, the scope, the decisions already made, and what has changed since the last stage.
- **Run the play's prompts in order, but validate between them.** Each output becomes input for the next only after you have checked it against real data.
- **Treat decision gates as client decisions.** The board prepares the options and the recommendation. The sponsor or steering committee decides. You facilitate.
- **Do not leave a stage until the exit criteria are met or consciously waived.** If you waive a criterion, record who agreed and why in the Context Brief.

| Stage | Lead advisors | Signature outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and assessment | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | Shared understanding of why, who, and what changes |
| Strategy and design | 4, 6, 7, 8, 10 | An integrated OCM strategy with measures |
| Build and preparation | 6, 7, 8 | Assets, networks, and training ready to deploy |
| Pilot and deployment | 4, 9 | A safe, evidence-based launch decision |
| Hypercare and sustained adoption | 9, 10 | Stable operations and proven adoption |

## Play 1: Discovery and assessment

### Objectives

- Establish why the change matters and how its value will be proved.
- Identify who is affected, who shapes success, and where resistance may arise.
- Understand what the change will feel like for each employee population.
- Build a traceable view of what changes and what could go wrong.
- Set the readiness framework and baseline.

### Lead advisors

1 Change Vision & Business Value Strategist, 2 Stakeholder Intelligence & Engagement Strategist, 3 Employee Experience & Change Journey Designer, 4 Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert, 5 Change Impact, Risk & Mitigation Architect.

### Key deliverables

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Validated with |
|---|---|---|
| Case for change, benefits map, WIIFM matrix | 1 | Sponsor, benefit owners |
| Stakeholder map and influence–interest grid | 2 | Program lead, trusted insiders |
| Illustrative personas and journey maps | 3 | Employee conversations or research |
| Readiness framework and baseline plan | 4 | Business unit leaders |
| Impact assessment, heatmap, risk register | 5 | Process owners |

### Decision gates

- **Gate 1a:** Sponsor confirms the case for change and the owners of each success measure.
- **Gate 1b:** Program leadership confirms scope, deployment assumptions, and the audiences in scope.

### Prompts

```text
Act as the Board Chair. We are entering discovery and assessment on
[program description]. Here is the Context Brief [version]. Route the work
to the right advisors, tell me in what order to run it, and list the real
data I must collect before any assessment can be treated as validated.
```

```text
Have the Change Impact, Risk & Mitigation Architect and the Employee
Experience & Change Journey Designer work together. Map the impacts below
to the persona hypotheses, identify the moments that matter for each
persona, and flag the impacts with no clear owner or response.
```

### Exit criteria

- [ ] Case for change confirmed; every benefit is evidenced or labeled as an assumption with an owner.
- [ ] Stakeholder map reviewed with the program lead; sponsor gaps identified.
- [ ] Persona hypotheses tested with real employees; traits updated or relabeled.
- [ ] Impact assessment validated with process owners for every in-scope function.
- [ ] Readiness framework agreed; baseline data collection under way.
- [ ] Context Brief updated with confirmed facts and open assumptions.

## Play 2: Strategy and design

### Objectives

- Turn discovery insight into one integrated OCM strategy.
- Design the champion network, communications, and learning strategies.
- Define readiness criteria by wave and the adoption measurement framework.
- Make sure every strategy uses the same assumptions about scope and timeline.

### Lead advisors

4 Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert, 6 Change Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist, 7 Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect, 8 Learning Strategy & Capability Development Architect, 10 Adoption Analytics & Change Insights Strategist.

### Key deliverables

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness criteria, thresholds, go/no-go approach | 4 | Impact assessment |
| Champion network design and profile | 6 | Stakeholder map |
| Communications strategy and messaging matrix | 7 | Audience segmentation, personas |
| Learning strategy and curriculum map | 8 | Impact assessment |
| Measurement framework and KPI dictionary | 10 | Case for change success measures |

### Decision gates

- **Gate 2a:** Sponsor approves the integrated OCM strategy and resourcing asks, such as champion time and training capacity.
- **Gate 2b:** Program leadership approves readiness criteria and go/no-go thresholds before build begins.

### Prompts

```text
Have the Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect, the Change
Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist, and the Learning Strategy &
Capability Development Architect develop a coordinated plan for [wave or
audience]. Show how communications, champion activity, and training are
sequenced, and flag any week where employees face too many asks at once.
```

```text
Board challenge: here is the integrated OCM strategy. Give the strongest
case for and against approving it, name the evidence that would resolve
the difference, and check that every deliverable uses the timeline and
scope in Context Brief [version].
```

### Exit criteria

- [ ] All strategy deliverables trace back to discovery outputs.
- [ ] No conflicting assumptions across deliverables (timeline, waves, audiences).
- [ ] Every success measure has a baseline plan, a target, and an owner.
- [ ] Readiness criteria are observable and approved.
- [ ] Sponsor has approved the strategy and resourcing.

## Play 3: Build and preparation

### Objectives

- Build the assets the strategy calls for: communications, training materials, champion onboarding.
- Mobilize the champion network and equip managers.
- Prepare people and the business for the pilot and first deployment.

### Lead advisors

6 Change Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist, 7 Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect, 8 Learning Strategy & Capability Development Architect.

### Key deliverables

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Validated with |
|---|---|---|
| Champion onboarding pack and effectiveness scorecard | 6 | Champions, line managers |
| Asset drafts, manager toolkit, FAQ | 7 | Client communications team |
| Facilitator materials, train-the-trainer plan | 8 | Client training lead, trainers |
| Readiness checkpoint report | 4 (supporting) | Business unit leaders |

### Decision gates

- **Gate 3a:** Client communications and brand owners approve the voice and assets. Until then, all drafts stay labeled provisional.
- **Gate 3b:** Training lead confirms that materials match the configured system and local requirements, including any legal or works-council checks the client must make.

### Prompts

```text
Act as the Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect. Draft a sponsor
announcement and manager cascade for the first deployment wave, in a
provisional voice. Base it on the approved messaging matrix. Do not invent
leader commitments; mark anything that needs sponsor approval.
```

```text
Act as the Learning Strategy & Capability Development Architect. Review
these facilitator materials against the impact assessment. Tell me which
impacts are not covered, where the method may not fit a country or role,
and how competence will be demonstrated rather than assumed from attendance.
```

```text
Act as the Change Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist. Draft the
champion onboarding pack for [country or BU]. Include responsibilities,
time commitment, cadence, escalation routes, and how we will judge whether
the network is working.
```

### Exit criteria

- [ ] Communications assets approved by client owners and scheduled.
- [ ] Champions onboarded with clear responsibilities and escalation routes.
- [ ] Trainers prepared; materials tested against the real system.
- [ ] Readiness checkpoint shows no unresolved critical gaps for the pilot scope.
- [ ] Context Brief updated with build decisions and changes.

## Play 4: Pilot and deployment

### Objectives

- Use a pilot to reduce uncertainty before scale-up.
- Make an evidence-based go/no-go decision for each wave.
- Prepare people and support for launch.

### Lead advisors

4 Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert, 9 Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist.

### Key deliverables

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot charter and success criteria | 9 | Define what the pilot must prove |
| Readiness heatmap and go/no-go report | 4 | Show readiness by wave with evidence |
| Launch checklist | 9 | Confirm people, support, and communications are in place |
| Hypercare model and severity definitions | 9 | Prepare stabilization support |

### Decision gates

- **Gate 4a:** Steering committee approves the pilot charter and success criteria.
- **Gate 4b:** Go/no-go for each wave, based on demonstrated readiness, not reported green status.
- **Gate 4c:** Scale-up decision after the pilot, based on the pilot's success criteria.

### Prompts

```text
Act as the Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist. Design a pilot charter
and hypercare exit criteria for our first deployment wave. Separate what
would count as a defect, a knowledge gap, a process failure, and an access
problem, and say who owns each.
```

```text
Board challenge: challenge my go-live readiness recommendation for [wave].
Give the strongest case for, the strongest case against, and the evidence
that would resolve it. Have the Business Readiness & Operational
Transition Expert and the Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist lead.
```

```text
Act as the Board Chair. Prepare the go/no-go decision memo for [wave],
including options (go, go with conditions, delay), the evidence for each,
the risks, and your recommendation. Separate facts from assumptions.
```

### Exit criteria

- [ ] Pilot success criteria evaluated with real data and lessons logged.
- [ ] Go/no-go decision made and recorded by the accountable client leader.
- [ ] Launch checklist complete; support and escalation routes confirmed.
- [ ] Hypercare team briefed on triage, severity, and exit criteria.

> **Watch out:** Technical go-live success is not proof of adoption. A system that is up and stable can still be used incorrectly or avoided. Keep the people-readiness evidence separate from technical readiness in every go/no-go discussion.

## Play 5: Hypercare and sustained adoption

### Objectives

- Detect friction quickly and stabilize adoption after launch.
- Distinguish people issues from technical defects and route each correctly.
- Measure whether the change is understood, adopted, and sustained.
- Connect adoption results to the benefits promised in the case for change.

### Lead advisors

9 Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist, 10 Adoption Analytics & Change Insights Strategist. Advisor 1 rejoins for the benefits story.

### Key deliverables

| Deliverable | Owner advisor | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hypercare triage log and lessons-learned log | 9 | Track and resolve post-launch friction |
| Adoption dashboard specification | 10 | Segment adoption by BU, geography, role, wave |
| Insight-to-action log | 10 | Turn findings into owned actions |
| Benefits report | 10 with 1 | Show progress against success measures |

### Decision gates

- **Gate 5a:** Hypercare exit, when agreed exit criteria are met.
- **Gate 5b:** Handover to business-as-usual ownership of adoption measures and reinforcement.
- **Gate 5c:** Adjust plans for later waves based on lessons learned.

### Prompts

```text
Act as the Adoption Analytics & Change Insights Strategist. Specify an
adoption dashboard with a KPI dictionary, thresholds, and the actions each
breach triggers. Pair each quantitative measure with a qualitative source,
and do not treat logins alone as evidence of adoption.
```

```text
Have the Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist and the Adoption
Analytics & Change Insights Strategist review the hypercare data below
from [wave]. Separate defects, knowledge gaps, process failures, and
access problems; recommend actions and owners; and tell me whether we are
on track to meet hypercare exit criteria.
```

```text
Act as the Board Chair. Summarize the lessons learned from [wave] and tell
me which deliverables for the next wave should change. Update the
recommendations consistently and list what must go into the Context Brief.
```

### Exit criteria

- [ ] Hypercare exit criteria met and signed off by the accountable client leader.
- [ ] Adoption measures owned by the business, with a review cadence.
- [ ] Reinforcement actions in place, such as manager coaching and refresher learning.
- [ ] Benefits tracking handed over with baselines, targets, and owners.
- [ ] Lessons learned recorded in the Context Brief and applied to later waves.

## Moving between plays

Real programs rarely move cleanly from one stage to the next. Phased deployments mean you may run Play 5 for wave 1 while running Play 3 for wave 2. When stages overlap:

- Keep one Context Brief, with a section per wave.
- Ask the Board Chair to check for conflicting assumptions whenever a wave's status changes.
- Feed lessons from earlier waves into later plays before their decision gates, not after.

```text
Act as the Board Chair. We are running [wave 1] in hypercare and [wave 2]
in build and preparation. Review the Context Brief and tell me where the
plans for the two waves conflict, which lessons from wave 1 should change
wave 2 now, and which decisions I need to raise with the sponsor.
```
