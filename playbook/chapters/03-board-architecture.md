# 3. Board Architecture

The board has a simple structure: ten specialist advisors organized into four groups, plus a Board Chair function that coordinates them. Every advisor works from the same Client Change Context Brief and the same Board Operating Charter. This chapter gives you the map: who is on the board, how they are grouped, which advisors lead at each lifecycle stage, who works with whom, and how to route a question to the right advisor.

## Four groups

The ten advisors are organized into four groups that follow the logic of a change engagement: understand the change, assess and mobilize, enable and launch, then measure and improve.

| Group | Focus | Advisors | The question the group answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Strategy and understanding | 1, 2, 3 | Why change, who matters, and what will it feel like? |
| B | Impact, readiness, and mobilization | 4, 5, 6 | What changes, can the business operate, and who carries it locally? |
| C | Enablement and launch | 7, 8, 9 | Do people know, can they do it, and will launch be safe? |
| D | Measurement and continuous improvement | 10 | Is it working, and what should we do next? |

> **Tip:** In executive conversations you may see a simpler three-part grouping: Understand (advisors 1–3), Assess and mobilize (4–6), and Enable and measure (7–10). It describes the same board. Use the four groups when you are working with the board and the three-part version when a slide needs to be simple.

## The Board Chair function

The Board Chair is the "Chief Change Strategist function." It is not an eleventh specialist and has no discipline of its own. It is the default operating mode of the board: whenever you bring a request without naming an advisor, the Board Chair handles it.

The Board Chair does three things:

| Responsibility | What it does | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Synthesizes advice | Routes your request to the right advisors and combines their input into one coherent response | One answer rather than ten separate opinions |
| Resolves conflicts between advisors | Surfaces disagreements, explains the trade-off, and names the evidence that would settle it | Visible trade-offs instead of hidden contradictions |
| Prepares executive decisions | Frames the decision, options, risks, and recommendation for a sponsor or steering committee | Decision-ready material you refine and present |

The Board Chair coordinates every lifecycle stage. It also protects consistency: if two advisors are working from different assumptions, the Chair should flag the conflict against the Context Brief before you see two incompatible drafts.

> **Watch out:** The Board Chair resolves conflicts between advisors by making the trade-off clear. It does not make the decision for you. When advisors disagree on something material, such as a go-live date or the load placed on frontline employees, the decision is yours to recommend and the client's to make. Chapter 8, "The Board Chair Playbook," gives the full method.

## Lifecycle stages and lead advisors

The Client Change Context Brief records which lifecycle stage your engagement is in. Each stage has indicative lead advisors. "Lead" means these advisors usually carry the most work at that stage; it does not limit who you can call. Every advisor can be called at any stage, and the Board Chair coordinates every stage.

| Advisor | Discovery and assessment | Strategy and design | Build and preparation | Pilot and deployment | Hypercare and sustained adoption |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Commercial Challenger | Lead | | | | |
| 2 Political Navigator | Lead | | | | |
| 3 Empathy Architect | Lead | | | | |
| 4 Operational Realist | Lead | Lead | | Lead | |
| 5 Systems Thinker | Lead | | | | |
| 6 Grassroots Mobilizer | | Lead | Lead | | |
| 7 Narrative Craftsman | | Lead | Lead | | |
| 8 Learning Engineer | | Lead | Lead | | |
| 9 Calm Incident Commander | | | | Lead | Lead |
| 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | | Lead | | | Lead |

Read the matrix both ways:

- **Down a column** to see who to involve when you enter a stage. Entering strategy and design, plan to work with advisors 4, 6, 7, 8, and 10.
- **Across a row** to see when an advisor's work peaks. The Operational Realist leads at three stages because readiness is defined early, designed into the strategy, and tested at deployment.

> **Tip:** Blank cells are not "do not call." The Commercial Challenger leads in discovery, but you will likely return to it when preparing a benefits report during sustained adoption. The Evidence-Based Investigator leads at strategy and hypercare, but you will want it in discovery when defining baselines.

## The collaboration network

Each advisor has defined "works with" partners: the advisors whose outputs they most depend on or most often feed. Use this table to set up cross-functional requests and to check that handoffs are happening.

| Advisor | Works with | Why they connect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Commercial Challenger | 2 Political Navigator, 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | Vision needs sponsor support; value claims need measures |
| 2 Political Navigator | 3 Empathy Architect, 6 Grassroots Mobilizer | Stakeholder segments inform personas and champion coverage |
| 3 Empathy Architect | 2 Political Navigator, 8 Learning Engineer, 7 Narrative Craftsman | Personas shape engagement, learning design, and messaging |
| 4 Operational Realist | 5 Systems Thinker, 9 Calm Incident Commander | Impacts define readiness criteria; readiness informs launch decisions |
| 5 Systems Thinker | 3 Empathy Architect, 4 Operational Realist, 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | Impacts trace to personas, feed readiness, and need measures |
| 6 Grassroots Mobilizer | 2 Political Navigator, 7 Narrative Craftsman | Champions need influence mapping and message support |
| 7 Narrative Craftsman | 1 Commercial Challenger, 3 Empathy Architect | Messages carry the vision to persona-specific audiences |
| 8 Learning Engineer | 5 Systems Thinker, 4 Operational Realist | Training needs come from impacts; capability evidence feeds readiness |
| 9 Calm Incident Commander | 4 Operational Realist, 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | Launch depends on readiness; stabilization depends on adoption signals |
| 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | 1 Commercial Challenger, 4 Operational Realist | Measures prove value and inform readiness decisions |

Two handoffs matter most early in an engagement:

- **Change impact feeds training and readiness.** The Systems Thinker's impact assessment is the main input for the Learning Engineer's training needs analysis and the Operational Realist's readiness criteria.
- **Stakeholder personas feed communications.** The Political Navigator's segments and the Empathy Architect's personas give the Narrative Craftsman its audiences.

**Check:** When a training plan or communications plan reaches you, ask which impact assessment or persona set it was built on. If the answer is "none," the handoff did not happen.

## How to pick an advisor

Start with the question you are trying to answer, not the deliverable you think you need. Use this routing table.

| If your question is... | Start with | Consider adding |
|---|---|---|
| Why are we doing this, and what is the business value? | 1 Commercial Challenger | 10 Evidence-Based Investigator |
| Who do I need on side, and where is support weakest? | 2 Political Navigator | 6 Grassroots Mobilizer |
| How will frontline employees experience this? | 3 Empathy Architect | 2 Political Navigator |
| Is the business ready to go live? | 4 Operational Realist | 9 Calm Incident Commander |
| What exactly is changing, for whom, and what could go wrong? | 5 Systems Thinker | 4 Operational Realist |
| How do we build local ownership across countries or sites? | 6 Grassroots Mobilizer | 7 Narrative Craftsman |
| What do we tell each audience, and who should say it? | 7 Narrative Craftsman | 3 Empathy Architect |
| Can people actually do the new work? | 8 Learning Engineer | 5 Systems Thinker |
| How do we run the pilot, launch, and stabilize? | 9 Calm Incident Commander | 4 Operational Realist |
| Is adoption happening, and what should we do about it? | 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | 1 Commercial Challenger |
| I need to prepare a decision for the sponsor | Board Chair | Advisors relevant to the decision |
| I am not sure which advisor I need | Board Chair | Let the Chair route |

Three routing rules keep you out of trouble:

1. **Start simple.** Use one advisor until you trust the output, then expand to cross-functional requests. Chapter 7, "Four Operating Modes," explains when to move up.
2. **Name the advisor when you know the discipline.** A precise request to the right specialist produces better work than a general request to the whole board.
3. **Use the Board Chair when the question crosses disciplines** or when you need a decision framed, not a deliverable drafted.

```text
Board Chair: I need to recommend to the steering committee whether Wave 1 of our fictional global ERP program should go live on [date]. Route this to the advisors you judge relevant, show me where they disagree, and prepare a one-page decision framing with options, risks, and the evidence still missing.
```
