# 3. Board Architecture

The board has ten specialist advisors in four groups, plus a coordinating Board Chair function. All work from the same Client Change Context Brief and Board Operating Charter.

## Four groups

The groups follow a change engagement: understand, assess and mobilize, enable and launch, measure and improve.

| Group | Focus | Advisors | The question the group answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Strategy and understanding | 1, 2, 3 | Why change, who matters, and what will it feel like? |
| B | Impact, readiness, and mobilization | 4, 5, 6 | What changes, can the business operate, and who carries it locally? |
| C | Enablement and launch | 7, 8, 9 | Do people know, can they do it, and will launch be safe? |
| D | Measurement and continuous improvement | 10 | Is it working, and what should we do next? |

## The ten advisors

Each nickname signals what the advisor will push you on; the core question defines their discipline.

| # | Advisor | Nickname | Group | Core question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Change Vision & Business Value Strategist | The Commercial Challenger | A | Why is this change worth doing, and how will we prove it created value? |
| 2 | Stakeholder Intelligence & Engagement Strategist | The Political Navigator | A | Who can enable or obstruct the change, and what will it take to earn their support? |
| 3 | Employee Experience & Change Journey Designer | The Empathy Architect | A | What will this change actually feel like for the people living through it? |
| 4 | Business Readiness & Operational Transition Expert | The Operational Realist | B | Can the business operate successfully when the change goes live? |
| 5 | Change Impact, Risk & Mitigation Architect | The Systems Thinker | B | What is changing, who must work differently, and what could go wrong? |
| 6 | Change Champion & Local Mobilization Strategist | The Grassroots Mobilizer | B | How do we create credible local ownership without overloading employees? |
| 7 | Strategic Communications & Narrative Architect | The Narrative Craftsman | C | What does each audience need to hear, from whom, through which channel, and when? |
| 8 | Learning Strategy & Capability Development Architect | The Learning Engineer | C | Can people perform the new work correctly, confidently, and independently? |
| 9 | Pilot, Deployment & Hypercare Strategist | The Calm Incident Commander | C | How do we launch safely, detect friction quickly, and stabilize adoption? |
| 10 | Adoption Analytics & Change Insights Strategist | The Evidence-Based Investigator | D | Are people adopting the change, what explains the results, and what should we do next? |

Part III (Chapters 9 to 18) gives each advisor a full chapter.

## The Board Chair function

The Board Chair is the "Chief Change Strategist function," not an eleventh specialist. Any request that names no advisor goes to the Chair.

| Responsibility | What it does | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Synthesizes advice | Routes requests and combines input into one response | One answer, not ten opinions |
| Resolves conflicts between advisors | Surfaces disagreements, explains trade-offs, names settling evidence | Visible trade-offs |
| Prepares executive decisions | Frames decision, options, risks, and recommendation for sponsors | Decision-ready material |

> **Watch out:** The Chair clarifies trade-offs; it does not decide. On material disagreements, you recommend and the client decides. See Chapter 8.

## Lifecycle stages and lead advisors

Each lifecycle stage has indicative lead advisors. Any advisor can be called at any stage; the Chair coordinates all of them.

| Lifecycle stage | Indicative lead advisors |
|---|---|
| Discovery and assessment | 1 Commercial Challenger, 2 Political Navigator, 3 Empathy Architect, 4 Operational Realist, 5 Systems Thinker |
| Strategy and design | 4 Operational Realist, 6 Grassroots Mobilizer, 7 Narrative Craftsman, 8 Learning Engineer, 10 Evidence-Based Investigator |
| Build and preparation | 6 Grassroots Mobilizer, 7 Narrative Craftsman, 8 Learning Engineer |
| Pilot and deployment | 4 Operational Realist, 9 Calm Incident Commander |
| Hypercare and sustained adoption | 9 Calm Incident Commander, 10 Evidence-Based Investigator |

> **Tip:** Absence from a stage is not "do not call." Bring the Evidence-Based Investigator into discovery to define baselines.

## The collaboration network

Use these "works with" partners to set up cross-functional requests and check handoffs.

| Advisor | Works with | Why they connect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Commercial Challenger | 2, 10 | Vision needs sponsors; value needs measures |
| 2 Political Navigator | 3, 6 | Segments inform personas and champion coverage |
| 3 Empathy Architect | 2, 8, 7 | Personas shape engagement, learning, and messaging |
| 4 Operational Realist | 5, 9 | Impacts define readiness; readiness informs launch |
| 5 Systems Thinker | 3, 4, 10 | Impacts trace to personas and readiness |
| 6 Grassroots Mobilizer | 2, 7 | Champions need influence mapping and message support |
| 7 Narrative Craftsman | 1, 3 | Messages carry the vision to persona-specific audiences |
| 8 Learning Engineer | 5, 4 | Impacts drive training; capability feeds readiness |
| 9 Calm Incident Commander | 4, 10 | Launch needs readiness; stabilization needs signals |
| 10 Evidence-Based Investigator | 1, 4 | Measures prove value and inform readiness decisions |

The critical early handoffs: impact assessment feeds training and readiness; personas feed communications. **Check:** ask what impact assessment or persona set each training or communications plan used. "None" means the handoff failed.

## How to pick an advisor

Start with your question, not the deliverable.

| If your question is... | Start with | Consider adding |
|---|---|---|
| Why are we doing this, and what is the business value? | 1 | 10 |
| Who do I need on side, and where is support weakest? | 2 | 6 |
| How will frontline employees experience this? | 3 | 2 |
| Is the business ready to go live? | 4 | 9 |
| What is changing, for whom, and what could go wrong? | 5 | 4 |
| How do we build local ownership across countries or sites? | 6 | 7 |
| What do we tell each audience, and who should say it? | 7 | 3 |
| Can people actually do the new work? | 8 | 5 |
| How do we run the pilot, launch, and stabilize? | 9 | 4 |
| Is adoption happening, and what should we do about it? | 10 | 1 |
| I need to prepare a decision for the sponsor | Board Chair | Advisors relevant to the decision |
| I am not sure which advisor I need | Board Chair | Let the Chair route |

Start with one advisor until you trust the output (see Chapter 7). Name the advisor when you know the discipline; use the Chair when the question crosses disciplines or needs a decision framed.

```text
Board Chair: I need to recommend to the steering committee whether Wave 1 of our fictional global ERP program should go live on [date]. Route to relevant advisors, show where they disagree, and prepare a one-page decision framing with options, risks, and missing evidence.
```
